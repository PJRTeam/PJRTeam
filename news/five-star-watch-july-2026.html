Five-Star Watch: July 2026

Two rating systems decide your census. Most operators only manage one.

From PJR Team — digital governance for skilled nursing and senior living operators

Every administrator knows the CMS Five-Star rating cold. Fewer can tell you their Google rating across their portfolio — or who actually controls the profiles it lives on. That gap is the story of 2026, because families no longer see these as two separate systems. They see one impression: “Is this a good facility?”

Here’s what changed this year, and what it means for your front door.

CMS tightened the methodology — again

The January 2026 refresh replaced the long-stay antipsychotic quality measure with a respecified version that pulls in Medicare and Medicaid claims data and Medicare Advantage encounter data, not just MDS self-reporting. Translation: less room for coding to carry a rating. Providers are now scored into ten equal deciles based on actual claims-verified performance.

That follows the 2025 shift to calculating health inspection ratings from the two most recent standard surveys instead of three — which means a single bad survey now moves your star rating faster than it used to.

And in June, CMS refreshed its Nursing Home Chain Performance Measures dataset, publishing average star ratings by chain — health inspection, staffing, and quality measures rolled up at the ownership level. If you operate multiple facilities, your portfolio’s reputation is now a public, downloadable spreadsheet.

Meanwhile, the other rating system got smarter

While CMS was tightening its data, Google and the AI search engines were tightening theirs. AI-powered answers now sit at the top of search results, and families increasingly ask tools like ChatGPT and Gemini direct questions — “which skilled nursing facility near me has the best rehab outcomes?” — instead of scrolling a list of links.

Those AI answers are built substantially from your reviews, your Google Business Profile data, and what’s being said about you in forums. Industry analysts are now advising operators to pursue review specificity — reviews that mention actual departments and experiences — because AI engines weight detailed, experience-rich reviews more heavily than a bare five stars.

The July watch list

Three things worth checking before the month is out:

  • Pull your chain-level CMS data. Referral sources and families can see it. Know what they’re seeing before your next hospital liaison meeting.
  • Audit Google Business Profile ownership across your portfolio. In our facility audits, unclaimed profiles, profiles owned by former vendors, and profiles controlled by ex-employees are the most common — and most dangerous — finding. You cannot manage a reputation on an asset you don’t control.
  • Compare your two star ratings. A 4-star CMS facility with a 3.1 Google rating is losing referrals to a 3-star competitor with a 4.6. Families check Google first. Discharge planners increasingly do too.

Where PJR Team fits

This is the exact intersection we work in. PJR Team helps SNF and senior living operators take legal ownership and governance of their Google Business Profiles, build review strategies designed for how AI search actually reads reviews, and respond to feedback in a way that’s HIPAA-compliant — because a well-meaning review reply that confirms a resident’s identity is a compliance incident, not a marketing win.

Your CMS rating is earned in the building. Your Google rating is earned online — but only if you govern it. If you don’t know who owns your profiles today, that’s the first conversation to have.
