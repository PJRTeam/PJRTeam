The phone isn’t the problem. The silence is.

The phone policy didn’t fail. The reporting chain did.

By Brittney Davis, Founder of PJR Team — published in McKnight’s Long-Term Care News, Marketplace Experts

This column was published in McKnight’s Long-Term Care News on October 22, 2026. Read the original on McKnight’s ↗

A CNA filmed a resident with severe cognitive impairment and shared it in a group chat.

At least six coworkers saw it. Nobody reported it for weeks.

When a witness finally did, she said she didn’t think it counted. The resident was fully clothed.

The state fined the home $500. Not for the video. For failing to report.

The home did what almost every building does: fired the aide, updated the phone policy, re-educated staff.

That isn’t wrong. It just isn’t enough.

CMS told surveyors 10 years ago that demeaning photos and recordings of residents are mental abuse. The policies have been on the books ever since. The violations keep showing up anyway, in at least 30 states.

The phone policy didn’t fail. The reporting chain did.

In my new McKnight’s column, I share three things that changed behavior across the 300+ frontline staff I’ve trained:

  1. Teach the line, not just the rule. “Any photo or video of a resident you wouldn’t want taken of your grandmother is abuse. Report it the same shift.”
  2. Make reporting a bystander duty, and make it safe. Track your report volume. Zero is not a good number.
  3. Give resident privacy a named owner, the way infection control has a preventionist.

No new tech. No big budget. Just an owner, a cadence and a number you watch.

Two questions for every administrator: Would your people recognize what they saw? And would they tell you?
