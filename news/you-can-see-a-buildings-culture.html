You Can See a Building’s Culture Before You Walk In

Families and job candidates don’t get those five minutes anymore. They form their first impression from a phone screen.

From PJR Team — for skilled nursing and senior living operators

Ask any seasoned administrator how they size up a facility, and they’ll tell you they know within five minutes of walking in. How the receptionist greets you. Whether the aide in the hallway makes eye contact with a resident. Whether anyone is laughing.

Families and job candidates don’t get those five minutes anymore. They form their first impression from a phone screen. They see the review that says a nurse stayed late to sit with Mom. They see the Facebook page that hasn’t posted since spring. They see the job listing, and the employee review underneath it.

Culture used to be something you felt in the building. Now it’s something people read about first. And in skilled nursing, culture isn’t a soft issue. It shows up in staffing, quality, and census.

Why culture is a clinical issue in skilled nursing

The clearest evidence is turnover. A landmark Health Affairs study of payroll data from more than 15,000 nursing homes found median annual staff turnover of 94% in 2017-2018. In plain terms, the typical building replaced nearly its entire workforce in a single year.

The gap between good and struggling buildings was stark. One-star facilities had median turnover of about 135%, compared with about 77% at five-star facilities.

Turnover isn’t only an HR cost. Every departure means a resident meets a new face, a routine gets relearned, and a family loses the person they trusted. Culture is what makes people stay: feeling respected, trained, and proud of where they work.

That pride, or the lack of it, is now public. When staff feel unprepared and unsupported, it leaks out in employee reviews, in frustrated posts, and sometimes in posts that should never have been made. When staff feel proud, it shows up too, in the family reviews that name an aide and in a social feed full of real moments.

Culture is built from the inside out

Most organizations try to build culture from the top down: a mission statement, a values poster, a corporate video. Those have their place. But residents and families don’t experience the mission statement. They experience the CNA at 6 a.m., the dietary aide at lunch, and the activities assistant on a Saturday.

Those frontline team members are your culture. They’re also your most trusted messengers. A real photo of a resident’s birthday celebration, taken with consent and shared the right way, says more about a building than any ad.

The catch is that most frontline staff have never been taught how to do that safely. They don’t know what consent looks like, what can appear in the background of a photo, or where the line sits between celebrating a moment and exposing a resident. So facilities face two bad options: lock everything down and go silent, or hope nothing goes wrong.

There’s a third option. Train people well enough that you can trust them.

How PJR Team helps

PJR Team works with skilled nursing and senior living operators to turn frontline staff into confident, compliant culture carriers. Our approach starts on the floor, not in the boardroom.

  • On-site training for every level. Sessions for frontline staff, facility leadership, and corporate marketing teams, delivered one time or on an ongoing schedule. We cover photo and video consent, HIPAA basics for social media, personal accounts, and AI tools, in plain language staff actually remember.
  • A culture people can see. We help facilities capture and share real moments safely, so the online presence reflects the building families will actually walk into.
  • Governance that protects it. We make sure the facility owns its Google Business Profile, website, and social accounts, and that reviews get thoughtful, HIPAA-compliant responses. A culture you’ve built shouldn’t disappear when one employee leaves.
  • Quarterly refreshers. Platforms and risks change fast, so training can’t be a one-time event.

The approach comes from experience. The clearest result: facilities began getting photo and video release forms signed, because staff finally understood why they mattered. Once people understood the rules, they could share the work they were proud of.

Culture is your best marketing

You can’t buy culture, and you can’t fake it online for long. But you can make sure the culture you’ve worked hard to build is visible, protected, and carried by the people who live it every shift.

The buildings that win the next decade on staffing and census won’t be the ones with the biggest ad budgets. They’ll be the ones whose own teams tell their story, and know how to tell it safely.
